## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Veteran-owned mortgage **broker** that forces 30+ wholesale lenders to bid on your loan — getting veterans and homebuyers a lower rate and a 14-day close, with no lender fees for veterans.
**What it does:** Future Home Loans is an independent mortgage brokerage, not a retail direct lender. Instead of one bank's rate sheet, they push each borrower's file through a pricing engine connected to 30+ wholesale lenders (incl. Products: FHA, VA, USDA, DSCR, HELOC, Fixed, ARM, Jumbo, Foreign National — purchase and refinance.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Getting a mortgage — especially a VA loan — through a retail giant or bank is slow, impersonal, and priced to the lender's advantage. The borrower subsidizes the lender's marketing budget through a higher rate, gets treated like a number, risks a blown closing date, and often isn't sure they'll qualify.
**Why alternatives fall short (see Competitive Landscape for mechanics):**
- Retail lenders sell **their own single rate sheet** — no shopping — with margins built to cover branches, management, and national advertising
- Bureaucratic lenders (credit unions, insurance-first) run **45–60 day** timelines that make a purchase offer unattractive and endanger the contract
- Automated retail underwriting issues **auto-denials** on complex veteran profiles a broker can manually place
- Builder/Realtor "preferred lenders" bury a **higher rate** (yield spread premium) inside a "free" closing-cost incentive
**What it costs them:** A worse rate for 15–30 years, thousands in avoidable origination fees, a delayed or dead deal, and — for the previously declined — the belief they can't own a home at all.
**Emotional tension:** "Will I even qualify?" · fear of being ghosted mid-deal with the moving truck packed · a veteran's frustration at not being helped by the brands that court them. Recurring review line: *"Never in a million years did I think I would be able to say, 'I'm a homeowner.'"*

**Mechanics (why they structurally can't match Future):**
- **Retail vs. wholesale margin:** wholesale originators (e.g. UWM) run ~106–133 bps gain-on-sale; retail lenders need 300+ bps to cover branches, management, and consumer-direct advertising. A broker accessing the wholesale channel is structurally cheaper.
- **Veterans United:** highest-volume VA lender, but sells its own proprietary debt (not a fiduciary shopping the market); avg origination fee ~$1,943 (HMDA), loans often sold to servicers like PennyMac.
- **Navy Federal / USAA:** operational bloat → 60-day timelines, unresponsive processing, missed closings.
- **Rocket:** ~0.10–0.25% higher rate to fund advertising; rigid AUS auto-denies bankruptcies, self-employment, high DTI.
- **Builder preferred lender:** the 30-year cost of the inflated rate exceeds the upfront "flex cash."

## Differentiation
*Ranked by what wins the deal on a sales call (client-confirmed). Note the re-rank: **COE pulling is table stakes, not a headline.***

1. **The broker shops 30+ wholesale lenders — retail literally can't.** The single most powerful differentiator, positioned as a structural reality. A bank LO is a W-2 employee with one rate sheet; a Future consultant forces 30+ wholesale lenders to bid on the veteran's file and accesses compressed wholesale margins. **Winning narrative:** *"A bank loan officer works for the bank and is paid to protect its margin. As a broker, I work for you — I make 30 banks bid against each other to win your mortgage. Retail banks can't do that; they can only sell you their own debt."*
2. **14-day average clear-to-close = the offer wins.** Speed sold as *security* — it solves the seller's fear of deal failure and makes the veteran's offer beat a 45–60 day competitor. Grounded in TRID mechanics (CD by ~day 11 → mandatory 3-day window → close day 14) and aggressive wholesale partners (UWM ~11-day CTC). Bureaucratic lenders physically can't. **Lead with this to Realtors.**
3. **No lender fees for veterans.** Tangible cash-to-close reduction vs. the ~$1,500–$3,000 origination fees retail charges (VA 1% cap). Requires nuance: **always** pair with "third-party costs (title, VA funding fee, escrow, recording) still apply" so it's not mistaken for "no closing costs."
4. **(Table stakes) We pull your COE.** Every VA-approved lender pulls the COE instantly via WebLGY — mention only as a pre-approval convenience ("no need to dig up your DD-214"), never as a primary reason to choose Future.

**Why customers choose us:** They were about to sign with a big name; someone told them to call Future; Future shopped it, beat the rate, waived the fees, treated them like family — and closed early.

## Anti-Persona
*Client-confirmed. Filter these out of paid lead-gen — they can't legally or mathematically close and drain LO bandwidth.*
1. **Sub-580 credit.** VA has no statutory minimum, but wholesale overlays effectively floor at ~580 (with strong compensating factors). Below that = exhaustive manual underwriting, high fallout. Qualify traffic to "580+"; route sub-580 to credit-repair resources, not the origination pipeline.
2. **Geographic misfit.** Any lead outside the 13-state footprint is unmonetizable. Geo-fence all SEO/paid search — and **exclude Arizona** (not licensed).
3. **Equity-deficient cash-out.** Recent purchase + no appreciation → fails the appraisal/LTV stage (conventional cash-out caps ~80% LTV; VA can go higher but overlays vary). Scrutinize debt-consolidation cash-out leads before ordering an appraisal.
4. **Mathematically disqualified VA IRRRL shopper.** Governed by 38 U.S.C. § 3709 — don't spend on or price these when they fail: **36-month recoupment** (recoupable costs ÷ monthly P&I savings ≤ 36; VA funding fee and prepaids excluded), **Net Tangible Benefit** (fixed→fixed rate drop ≥ 50 bps; fixed→ARM ≥ 200 bps), **seasoning** (6 consecutive payments AND 210 days since first payment due date). Never target veterans who bought < 210 days ago.
