## Article

Flagler College requires most first- and second-year students to live on campus. That means **off-campus housing searches spike for juniors, seniors, and eligible transfers** — usually in the spring and early summer before the academic year.

If that is you, you are not just picking a vibe. You are picking a commute, a utility strategy, and a lease that survives roommate drama.

### Flagler’s baseline rule (so you do not waste tours)

Per Flagler’s housing guidance:

- Incoming first-year students generally live on campus unless Admissions approves an exception.  
- Transfers with fewer than 24 semester hours generally live on campus unless approved.  

Confirm your eligibility with Flagler before putting down deposits anywhere. Marvento is **not affiliated with Flagler College**.

### The three off-campus paths Flagler students usually see

**1. Downtown / near-campus houses and duplexes**  
Pros: Walkability, historic neighborhood energy, roommate culture.  
Cons: High security deposits, unfurnished units, street parking stress, utilities split unevenly.

**2. Conventional apartment / townhome communities**  
Pros: Newer finishes, amenities, clearer management.  
Cons: Income multiples, guarantors, 12-month norms, fees that do not appear in ads.

**3. Furnished private rooms in a shared home**  
Pros: One all-inclusive number, private bath options, faster move-in, flexible terms.  
Cons: Shared kitchen and living spaces; fewer total rooms (tour early).

### What to prioritize within ~15 minutes of King Street

1. **Lease end date aligned to May / December** — not an arbitrary anniversary.  
2. **Private bathroom if you can swing it** — the #1 roommate conflict reducer.  
3. **In-unit or on-site laundry** — downtown laundry runs eat evenings.  
4. **A real desk** — cafes are for coffee, not every paper.  
5. **Total monthly outflow** — rent + utilities + Wi-Fi + parking + furniture.
